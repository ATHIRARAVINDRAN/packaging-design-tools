# How to choose a dieline tool

Start with the file the next person needs. “Packaging design” can mean structural engineering, artwork placement, client presentation or a cutting-table handoff. A good match for one task may leave another unfinished.

## 1. Write the receiving requirements

Ask the converter for the accepted structure, material, caliper, measurement convention and file format. Record whether the job needs inside printing, special finishes or several separate parts. Put those requirements beside the product measurements before selecting software.

## 2. Choose the workflow

| Requirement | First tool to evaluate | Reason for the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Browser-based structure, artwork and folded preview | [DielineMaker](https://dielinemaker.com/) | Connected parametric and visual workflow |
| Simple sized paper blank | [Templatemaker](https://www.templatemaker.nl/en/) | Focused template collection |
| Browser mockup and composed product scene | [Pacdora](https://www.pacdora.com/) | Presentation-oriented tools and model selection |
| Structural CAD within a converter's systems | [ArtiosCAD](https://www.esko.com/en/products/artioscad) | Dedicated engineering capabilities and interfaces |
| Desktop rendering of products and packaging | [Boxshot](https://appsforlife.com/boxshot/) | Customizable product shapes and scenes |

These are starting points, not a universal ordering of quality. Test the actual structure and receiving format.

## 3. Check geometry and access separately

In DielineMaker, [dimension modes and structural options](https://dielinemaker.com/learn/templates-and-dimensions) affect what the measurements mean. Change a dimension and inspect the new closure and panel relationships. Do not assume resizing is equivalent to scaling a finished drawing.

Then check whether the required output is included. DielineMaker's Free and Lite plans do not download dieline or production print files; those need Pro or Business. [Pricing and export entitlements](https://dielinemaker.com/pricing).

## 4. Use a small acceptance trial

Try a real product, representative artwork and the intended board. Inspect the exported scale and assemble a physical sample. Ask the receiving team to open the file and identify cut, crease and artwork correctly.

**What should decide the purchase?** The tool should finish the receiving workflow with acceptable files and a repeatable process. An attractive preview alone does not establish that.
